# Little Math Detectives (Thám tử Toán học Tí hon) — Game Descriptions

An interactive web app for **grade 1 students (6 years old)** in Vietnam. Children help the cat detective **Mèo Smarty** solve "cases" (vụ án), and each case is a small math game. Six games cover counting, number sense, addition and subtraction within 10 (later 20), and early logical reasoning.

The app runs in the browser on a landscape iPad or a computer. It needs no account and no internet connection once loaded.

---

## 1. How the app works for a child

### 1.1 Homepage

- Mèo Smarty greets the child by name and shows their **total stars** and **day streak**.
- Six big picture tiles, one per game, in a 3 × 2 grid. A tile for a game that is not ready yet shows "Sắp ra mắt" (coming soon).
- A speaker button turns sound on and off. A small **padlock** in the bottom-right corner (hold it for about 1 second) leads to the teacher area (PIN protected).

### 1.2 Who is playing?

Several children can share one device. At the start, each child taps their own **avatar** (a friendly picture, no password). Stars, streak, difficulty and time used are all kept **per child**.

### 1.3 A play session

- One session = **one set of 5 questions** in one game.
- At the top of the screen, five dots show progress through the set, and a label shows the current tier ("Vòng 1 / 2 / 3").
- Every question is **read aloud** by Mèo Smarty. A speaker button repeats it ("nghe lại").
- The child answers, then taps the green **"Xong"** button (or the answer is checked as soon as they finish the action, depending on the game).

### 1.4 The guide button (i)

Every game has an **(i)** button. Mèo Smarty then shows a worked example with a moving hand and a voice, solving one question step by step.

- It plays **automatically the first time a child opens a game**.
- The child can replay it at any time.
- Some of these games are hard for 6-year-olds, so this guide is important.

### 1.5 When the child is wrong

1. Mèo Smarty says something kind and gives a **hint**.
2. The child gets **one more try**.
3. Only the **first try** counts towards the score. The second try is for learning.

Wrong answers are never punished with scary sounds or red crosses. The tone is always "thử lại nhé!" (try again).

## 2. Difficulty: three rounds that adapt

### 2.1 The three rounds

| Round | Feel |
| --- | --- |
| **Vòng 1** | Warm-up: small numbers (0–5), pictures to help, one thing to think about |
| **Vòng 2** | Medium: numbers up to 10, fewer helpers, two things to think about |
| **Vòng 3** | Challenge: numbers up to 20 where it makes sense, little help, more steps |

Inside each round there are also **small hidden steps** (for example, numbers up to 5, then up to 8, then up to 10). Children never see these steps; the app uses them to make the game feel smooth.

### 2.2 Changing difficulty during a set

Within a set of 5 questions the app adjusts the difficulty after each answer:

- **Right answer** → counts towards moving up.
- **Wrong answer** → the child must answer **one extra question** correctly at the same round before moving up (a "careful" penalty).
- **Vòng 2 always has at least 2 questions** before the child can reach Vòng 3.
- **Two wrong answers in a row** → the child steps back down one round.

Reaching Vòng 3 is meant to be **hard and to take several days**. This rewards careful thinking, not speed.

### 2.3 Starting point for the next set

The next set starts from where the last set ended:

- 4 or 5 correct out of 5 → start one step higher.
- 0 or 1 correct → start one step lower.
- Otherwise → start at the same place.

Because of the daily time limit (see 4), a child will usually play **about one set per game per day**.

---

## 3. Stars and streak

### 3.1 Stars per set

| Result | Stars |
| --- | --- |
| Finished the set (tried their best) | ⭐ |
| 3 or more correct out of 5 | ⭐⭐ |
| 5 correct out of 5 | ⭐⭐⭐ |

Since the app pushes each child towards questions they find hard, nearly every child will get some answers wrong. So a star is given **for finishing**, and extra stars are given for doing well.

### 3.2 Total stars

All stars add up to a running total, shown on the homepage. (Optional: spend stars on hats and stickers for Mèo Smarty.)

### 3.3 Daily streak

- Earning **at least 1 star in a day** keeps the streak going.
- The message is always positive: "Hôm nay em đã cố gắng rồi!" (You did your best today!).
- Weekends do not break the streak (proposal; to be confirmed with teachers).

---

## 4. Screen time and healthy play

All limits are set per child in the teacher area and protected by a **PIN**. Parents or teachers can unlock more time with the PIN.

| Rule | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Session limit** (e.g. 15 minutes) | Mèo Smarty suggests a break ("nghỉ mắt một chút nhé") and shows a break screen with a countdown. |
| **Daily limit** (e.g. 30 minutes) | A warning appears when 2 minutes are left. After that the games are closed until tomorrow. |
| **Inactivity** | After about 20 seconds without touching the screen, Mèo Smarty gives a gentle nudge or hint. After about 60 seconds the game pauses. Time spent idle **does not count** against the limits. |
| **Never cut off mid-question** | If time runs out during a set, the child may finish the current set first. |

---

## 5. Sound and voice

- Everything the child needs to understand is **spoken** (6-year-olds are still learning to read).
- Voice clips are recorded by the **teacher** from a script, so the voice is familiar and the Vietnamese tones are correct. Numbers, operations, praise and warnings are recorded as short reusable pieces and joined together as needed.
- A speaker button turns all sound on and off. When sound is off, a **text panel** shows the instruction in words and pictures (needed for game 3).
- Sound effects are gentle and short.

## 6. The six games

Each game below lists: the idea, how it is played, the three rounds, and how answers are checked.

### 6.1 Cái cân — The Balance Scale

**Idea.** Fruit have different weights. Children learn that "equal" means *the same total weight*, not necessarily the same number of items. This is an early step towards addition, swapping and equivalence.

**Weights (a key is always shown on screen).**

- 1 nho (grape) = 1
- 1 táo (apple) = 2
- 1 dưa hấu (watermelon) = 3

A later variant uses **shapes**: a triangle weighs 3, a square or rectangle weighs 4. (Weights by colour were considered and dropped, because they would be an arbitrary rule to memorise.)

**How it is played.** Two pans of a scale each hold some fruit. A tray below holds more fruit. The child **drags fruit from the tray onto a pan** (or taps a fruit and then taps the pan) so that **both pans weigh the same**, then taps "Xong". The scale arm tilts to show the result.

**Rounds.**

| Round | What it looks like | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vòng 1 | One kind of fruit. The scale tips **live** as fruit is added, each pan shows its count, and a **>, < or = sign** above the pivot compares the two pans (5 > 2), linking the scale to the symbols children learn in grade 1. | 5 grapes against 2 grapes: add 3 grapes. |
| Vòng 2 | Two kinds of fruit, with the key shown. The scale tips **only after "Xong"**. | Left: 2 apples (4). Right: 1 apple + 1 grape (3): add 1 grape. |
| Vòng 3 | Three kinds of fruit (and, later, shapes). Bigger totals. The scale tips only after "Xong". | Left: 1 watermelon + 2 apples (7). Right: 2 watermelons (6): add 1 grape. |

**Checking.** The game compares the **total weight** of the two pans. Many different arrangements can be right (for example, 1 apple or 2 grapes both add 2). A wrong answer makes the scale tip the wrong way and Mèo Smarty hints which side is heavier.

### 6.2 Rô-bốt — The Robot

**Idea.** The first steps of programming and logical thinking. Children give the robot a list of arrow commands to reach the flag.

**How it is played.** A square grid shows the robot and a flag. The child taps arrow buttons (↑ ↓ ← →) to build a list under **"Các bước của em"** (my steps). They tap **"Chạy!"** (Run) and the robot walks the list one step at a time. **"Làm lại"** clears the list. Small helpers: tapping the **last** step takes it back (so one slip does not mean starting over), the computer's arrow keys also work, and pressing "Chạy!" with no steps only gives a gentle nudge; it does not count as a wrong try. A program holds up to 12 steps.

**Rounds.**

| Round | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Vòng 1 | Small grid (4 × 4), no obstacles, 2–3 steps in a straight or one-turn path. |
| Vòng 2 | **Walls** between squares and **blocked squares** the robot can never enter, so the path must go around them. |
| Vòng 3 | Longer paths with more turns and more obstacles. |

**Extra modes, if time allows.**

- **Number path (Vòng 2 and up):** the grid is filled with numbers. One path is a pattern (counting by 1s: 1, 2, 3…; by 2s: 2, 4, 6…; later by 10s). The child picks the path that follows the pattern. The correct squares light up **green**; a wrong square lights up **red** and the robot goes back to the start. In its easiest form the child taps the squares directly instead of using arrows.
- **Collect 10 (Vòng 3):** some squares add (+) or subtract (−) from the robot's "battery". The child must reach the flag with exactly 10. A running total is shown during the walk. (This replaces the idea of "find the shortest path", which is hard to judge.)

**Checking.** The robot actually walks the program. It succeeds if it ends on the flag. If it bumps into a wall or leaves the grid, it makes a funny "oops" move, and returns to the start, and Mèo Smarty gives a hint. The levels are made by the computer, and each one is checked to make sure a solution exists.

### 6.3 Giỏ trái cây — Give Me the Fruit

**Idea.** Listening, counting and moving exactly the right amount. A "call to action" game.

**How it is played.** Two baskets are on screen. One is full of fruit. Mèo Smarty says (for example) **"Đưa cho tôi ba quả cam"** (Give me three oranges). The child **drags** exactly three oranges from the full basket into the other basket, then taps "Xong". Too many is wrong, too few is wrong. Fruit can be dragged back.

**Rounds.**

| Round | What it looks like | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vòng 1 | A **small** number of **one kind** of fruit | "Đưa cho tôi 4 quả cam." |
| Vòng 2 | A **large** number of one kind. Fruit come in **bags of 10 (túi 10 quả = 1 chục)**, so children learn tens. | "Đưa cho Mèo hai chục quả cam" = 2 bags<br>"Đưa cho Mèo 13 quả cam" = 1 bag + 3 oranges |
| Vòng 3 | **Several kinds** of fruit at once. 0 can appear. | "Đưa cho tôi 2 quả cam và 3 quả táo." / "Đưa cho tôi 0 quả chuối." |

**Learning tens (chục) in Vòng 2.** The Vòng 2 questions are drawn on purpose from three kinds, so children meet "chục" again and again:

| Kind | Mèo says | Answer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whole tens | "một chục quả cam", "hai chục quả cam" | 1 bag / 2 bags | Links the word **chục** to one bag of 10. |
| Same amount, the other name | "mười quả cam" | 1 bag | Shows that **mười** and **một chục** are the same amount. |
| Tens and ones | "mười ba quả cam", "mười sáu quả cam" | 1 bag + 3 / 1 bag + 6 | Splits a number into tens and ones. |

About a third or more of the Vòng 2 questions are whole tens.

- **After a correct answer**, Mèo names the amount both ways, e.g. "Hai chục là 20 quả cam" or "Một chục và 3 quả là 13 quả cam."
- **After a wrong answer**, the hint reminds the child that each bag is 1 chục = 10 oranges.
- **In the picture panel**, a ten is drawn as a small bag marked "10", so the panel uses the same idea.
- **The child's basket never holds ten loose oranges.** It has 2 to 9 single oranges (always enough for the "ones") and 2 bags, so a chục can only be made by taking a bag; counting out ten single oranges is not possible.

**If the child can't hear (or doesn't understand).**

- A **text and picture panel** shows the request (for example, "3 🍊": the number with its picture, so a child who struggles with words can still read the number; in Vòng 2 the bags are shown underneath, e.g. "[10] + 3 🍊"). It is **always shown when the sound is off**; when the sound is on it appears after the **first wrong try**, before the child tries again.
- A **"nghe lại"** button repeats the request at any time.

**Counts on the baskets.** In Vòng 1, each basket shows how many fruit it holds, updating as the child moves fruit, to link the amount to the number. From Vòng 2 the counts are hidden, so the child has to count by themselves.

**Checking.** The basket must hold exactly the right number of each kind of fruit.

### 6.4 Tính theo hình — Fruit Equations

**Idea.** Each fruit stands for a number. Children add the fruit and later work backwards to find the value of a fruit. This introduces the idea of a *symbol standing for a number*, the first step towards algebra, in a gentle form.

**How it is played.** The screen shows fruit with values and equations built from fruit pictures. The child types the answer on a large **number pad**.

**Rounds.**

| Round | What it looks like | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vòng 1 | **Forward**, values shown. | 🍎 = 1. What is 🍎 + 🍎? → **2**<br>🍐 = 2, 🍎 = 1. What is 🍐 + 🍎? → **3** |
| Vòng 2 | **Longer sums** or **working backwards**. | 🍎 = 1, 🍐 = 2, 🍉 = 3. What is 🍎 + 🍐 + 🍉? → **6**<br>🍎 + 🍐 = 3 and 🍎 + 🍎 = 2. What is 🍐? → **2** |
| Vòng 3 | **Chains** of clues. | 🍎🍎🍎 = 6, 🍎 + 🍐 = 3, 🍎 + 🍐 + 🍉 = 6. What is 🍉? → **3** |

**Rule for the puzzles.** Every puzzle can be solved **one clue at a time**: at each step there is a clue with only one unknown fruit. The child finds it, replaces it with its number, and moves on. Puzzles never need two clues solved together. The computer makes the puzzles and checks that there is only **one** possible answer. Fruit can be worth **0**.

**Note.** This is the hardest game for this age. It uses the same fruit values as the balance scale so children see the same idea twice, and the adaptive rounds keep children from being placed on puzzles that are too hard.

### 6.5 Mấy và mấy — Splitting and Joining

**Idea.** Matches the grade 1 lesson "Mấy và mấy". A number can be split into two parts (7 = 3 + 4), and two parts can be joined into a whole. This is the foundation of addition and subtraction.

**How it is played.**

- **Picture form.** A vase of flowers (or a basket of fruit, or a plate of sweets) holds items of two kinds, e.g. white and yellow flowers. The sentence is read aloud: **"Trong lọ có [ ] bông hoa, trong đó có [ ] hoa trắng và [ ] hoa vàng."** Each blank has a small picture of its flower beside it. The child fills in the blanks on a number pad.
- **Number form (number bond).** Three boxes: one on top (the whole) joined by lines to two boxes beneath it (the parts). The top number equals the two lower numbers added together. One box is hidden, and the child works out what number it is.

**Rounds.**

| Round | What it looks like | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vòng 1 | Picture vase, **one blank**, usually the total (so, just counting). | A vase with 3 white and 2 yellow flowers. "Trong lọ có [ ] bông hoa." → **5** |
| Vòng 2 | Picture vase **plus the number bond**; **two blanks**. 0 can appear. | "Có 6 bông hoa. [ ] hoa trắng, [ ] hoa vàng." (white flowers shown: 4) → yellow = **2** |
| Vòng 3 | **Number bond only**, a missing part (secretly subtraction), larger numbers. | 7 on top, 3 on the left, [ ] on the right → **4** |

**Helpers for counting.** Items differ by **shape** as well as colour (so colour-blind children can tell them apart). Tapping an item marks it as counted, so it is not counted twice.

**Checking.** The numbers must make the whole equal the sum of the parts.

### 6.6 So sánh cân — Scale Detective (a stretch goal)

**Idea.** Reasoning from clues. Children look at pictures of scales and work out something that is not shown directly: if A is lighter than B, and B is lighter than C, then C is the heaviest.

**How it is played.** Two or three small scale pictures each compare two objects. The objects are of **similar size and kind** (for example, boxes of different colours, or equal-sized fruit), so the child cannot guess from real-world experience; the scales are the only clue. The child answers by **tapping the heaviest** object (out of three) or by **putting the objects in order from lightest to heaviest**. There are no yes/no questions, as a guess is right half the time.

**Rounds.**

| Round | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Vòng 1 | 3 objects, 2 scales. |
| Vòng 2 | 4 objects, 3 scales. |
| Vòng 3 | The scales are shown in a different order, or turned the other way round, so the child must read them carefully. |

**Note.** This kind of chained reasoning is at the upper limit of what 6-year-olds can do. It is built last. If it is not ready, the homepage shows a "Sắp ra mắt" (coming soon) tile.

## 7. The teacher area (khu vực giáo viên)

Reached through a small **padlock** in the bottom-right corner of the homepage (and the break screens). The padlock must be **held for about 1 second** (a ring fills up); a quick tap only shows the hint "Giữ để mở · dành cho người lớn". Then a PIN is asked. The area lets a teacher or parent:

- add, rename and remove child profiles
- set the session limit and daily limit for each child
- see each child's stars, streak and the round they have reached in each game
- **export a backup** of all progress to a file, or **import** one (so progress is not lost if the browser clears its storage)
- change the PIN
- choose the **effects** (see 8.1): Yên tĩnh (calm), Vừa phải (medium) or Rực rỡ (lively), or switch each effect on or off

All settings, including the effects, live **only** here, behind the PIN, so children cannot change them. The PIN keeps children out; it is not real security against an adult who knows computers.

## 8. Look and feel

- Bright, cartoon-style pictures (fruit, baskets, vases, a robot, Mèo Smarty). Pictures are rounder and more detailed than the early mockups, as the teachers asked.
- Each game has its own soft background colour, as in the mockups (yellow for the scale, green for the robot).
- No animals other than Mèo Smarty (no cages or aquariums).
- Large buttons that are easy for small fingers. Everything that can be dragged can also be **tapped** (tap the item, then tap where it goes).

### 8.1 Effects ("eye candy")

The screens stay tidy, and they **react** to the child. Four effects can each be switched on or off in the teacher area:

| Effect | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Hoạt ảnh** (animation) | Mèo Smarty blinks and bobs and jumps for joy; cards and tiles pop in; a picked-up fruit wiggles; the current progress dot pulses. |
| **Trang trí nền** (background) | A few faint pictures drift slowly at the edges of each screen. |
| **Pháo hoa** (celebration) | Confetti on a correct answer; sparkles when a fruit lands. |
| **Âm thanh hiệu ứng** (sound effects) | Short tap, pick-up, drop, success and "oops" sounds, separate from the voice. |

Three presets: **Yên tĩnh** (all off, for children who are easily distracted), **Vừa phải** (no background pictures), **Rực rỡ** (everything on, the default). If the device asks for reduced motion, animation starts off.

- Works on iPad in landscape and on desktop. On a phone or an upright iPad, a message asks the child to turn the screen sideways or use a tablet.
